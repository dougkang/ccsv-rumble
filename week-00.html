Draft Night 2026-27: Jokić Goes First, Steph Goes Nineteenth, and Everybody Goes Home Convinced They Won

One hundred and eighty picks, ten rounds, eighteen teams, and exactly zero games played — which means right now every single roster in this league is undefeated and gorgeous. Soak it in. The top of round one went chalk-adjacent: Jokić off the board at one, Wembanyama at two, and then a guard run that would make a point guard blush — Luka third, Shai fourth, Cade fifth. LeBron went sixth to the team literally named after being too tall, Giannis seventh, and by pick ten the ball-handlers were nearly picked clean. The fun stuff happened at the turn. Woo-Tang 4eva sat on back-to-back picks at eighteen and nineteen and walked away with Jalen Brunson and Stephen Curry, which is a tidy way to make the eighteenth pick feel like the second. Cooper Flagg hung around until fifteenth. Joel Embiid waited until the first pick of round four, the fifty-fifth selection overall. Then came the big-man stampede. Somewhere around rounds four through eight this draft turned into a center buffet — Zubac, Clingan, Gobert, Jarrett Allen, Poeltl, Hartenstein, Gafford, Vučević — and the boards got crowded in a hurry. If you waited on rebounds, you waited too long. If you took three centers, well, that's a choice, and we've got notes.

1. Woo-Tang 4eva new

Cash Rules Everything Around Me — Curry, Curry, y'all. Ranked first on paper after turning the eighteenth and nineteenth picks into Brunson and Steph, then doubling back for Dybantsa and Embiid at the round-three/four turn, and the Gordon-Brooks-Porziņģis-Caruso depth gives them something at basically every spot. Protect ya neck, and protect that frontcourt health. Grade: A.

2. Elsie’s World new

Elsie's World — we're all just drafting in it. Shai at four is the kind of anchor you build a decade around, and pairing Mobley with Jaren Jackson Jr. at picks thirty-three and forty is a defensive front line with real teeth. Pritchard, Braun, and DeRozan are nice rotation glue, though the backcourt leans hard on one superstar. Grade: A.

3. The Wongster new

Two Wongs don't make a right, but one Wongster made a very tidy draft. Flagg at fifteenth and Scottie Barnes at twenty-second is a young, position-flexible core, and Kyrie plus Coby White gives the backcourt some juice. Ace Bailey and Mikel Brown Jr. in the last two rounds is a lot of upside stacked behind an already youthful roster — fun, if the development curves cooperate. Grade: A-.

4. WEE-TOO-TALL new

WEE-TOO-TALL and they absolutely drafted like it. LeBron at six and Anthony Davis at thirty-first, then Siakam, Sharpe, Claxton, Gafford, and Diabaté — that is a frontcourt you could build a cathedral ceiling with. Herro, Dylan Harper, and Jeremiah Fears carry the guard load, so the assists will need to come from somewhere tall. Grade: A-.

5. Triton Aggies new

Luka at three, then a very balanced grocery run. Bam and Brandon Miller back-to-back at thirty-fourth and thirty-ninth, Edey for the glass, and Josh Hart, NAW, and Herb Jones for the dirty-work categories. Sheppard in round nine is a lovely late swing if the minutes open up. Grade: B+.

6. 🥭 smoothie’s team new

Everything goes in the blender when you've got Jokić on top — first overall, no hesitation, perfectly smooth. Duren and Harden at thirty-six and thirty-seven is a lovely pairing, and Ausar Thompson plus Suggs loads up on the pesky-defender categories; Naz Reid and Queta make for a very mango-thick center rotation. Grade: A-.

7. Keyboard Cats new

The Keyboard Cats mashed the keys and somehow typed out Giannis and Booker. Kessler at forty-three and Alex Sarr at sixty-six means blocks will not be an issue, though three true bigs plus Giannis is a lot of beef for one lineup card. Nembhard, VanVleet, and Anthony Black are steady hands behind Booker. Grade: B+.

8. The best wongster new

The best Wongster says the proof is in the pudding, and the pudding is three centers. KAT at sixteen and Trae at twenty-one is a monster offensive duo, then Zubac at fifty-seven and Poeltl at one-twenty-four stacked the middle high; Powell, Barrett, and Mathurin give the wings plenty of shot volume. Somebody's going to spend Sunday nights shuffling that frontcourt. Grade: B+.

9. Peter Piper Picked a Pick&Roll new

Peter Piper picked a peck of pick-and-roll partners. Ant at ten and Chet at twenty-seven is a gorgeous one-two, and adding Gobert at eighty-two means the blocks column could get downright unfair. Derrick White, Jrue, and Tari Eason round out a defense-first build that will need the shooting to hold up. Grade: A-.

10. pau-erful slacker new

Pau-erful indeed, and not remotely slacking. Tatum at nine, Jalen Williams at forty-five, Cameron Boozer at sixty-four and Paul George at eighty-one is a forward collection with no shortage of scoring, and Jamal Murray is the lone high-end ball-handler. Cason Wallace and Grimes help the steals, but the guard slots are leaning on a lot of forwards wearing name tags. Grade: B+.

11. The Lou Whoppers new

The Lou Whoppers ordered it with everything on it — Wembanyama second overall, then Markkanen and Sabonis at thirty-five and thirty-eight. That's a triple-stack of bigs before round four was over, and Fox plus Rollins and Tre Jones have to cover all the backcourt bun duty. Hold the guards, apparently. Grade: B+.

12. California Burritos new

California Burritos, fully loaded, extra everything, no room to close the foil. Jalen Johnson, Amen Thompson, and Paolo in the first three rounds is a terrific young core, and then Jarrett Allen, Jabari, Maluach, Nurkić, and Raynaud piled the rebounds on until the tortilla split. The threes will need to come from Garland and friends. Grade: B+.

13. #14th Scariest Team new

The #14th Scariest Team came out of the turn with Donovan Mitchell and Kevin Durant at seventeen and twenty, which is not actually very scary — it's terrifying. Clingan, Zion, and Myles Turner add real frontcourt pop, and drafting two different Georges (Keyonte and Kyshawn) is the kind of roster that will cause at least one lineup mix-up by Thanksgiving. Grade: A-.

14. Lebron’s Coach new

LeBron's Coach drafted a whole team and not one LeBron — bold scheme. Maxey at eight with Trey Murphy and Bane behind him is a lovely wing-and-shooting build, and Podziemski plus Acuff keeps the guard well full. Murray-Boyles and Draymond are the closest thing to a center, so the rebounding plan will need some inspiration. Grade: B.

15. Cade and friends new

Cade and friends took Cade at five, which is the most on-brand pick in league history and should be framed. Giddey at thirty-two and Kawhi at forty-one give them passing and two-way punch, then Ware, Collins, Missi, and Vučević turned the back half into a center convention. The friends are many; the shooters are fewer. Grade: B.

16. Isaac Destroyer of Worlds new

Isaac, Destroyer of Worlds, apparently also destroyer of the center position. LaMelo, Jaylen Brown, LaVine, Ja Morant, Jaquez, Klay, and Hardaway is an enormous pile of guard scoring and threes, and Jaden McDaniels and Peyton Watson are left holding the rebounding bag. If the assists and triples land, nobody will care; if not, the glass gets lonely. Grade: B.

17. Willy the baller new

Willy the baller went and balled out at eleven with Haliburton, then added Avdija and Franz Wagner for a wonderfully well-rounded wing group. Castle, Jalen Green, and McCain stack the backcourt, while Mitchell Robinson, Caleb Wilson, and Bassey are the frontcourt gamble — upside everywhere, floor to be determined. Grade: B.

18. God's Favorite Danny new

God's Favorite Danny was favored enough to get Şengün at fourteen and Austin Reaves at twenty-three, which is a sneaky-good opening hymn. Dyson Daniels and Davion Mitchell make this the steals capital of the league, and Lillard, Quickley, and Butler give the roster plenty of bounce-back upside. Ranked last on paper is just a dare, and the back rounds of this draft look like the answer. Grade: B.

So that's the board. Eighteen rosters, a hundred and eighty names, and about four hundred group-chat opinions already flying. Week one is when the fun actually starts: lineups lock, somebody forgets to slot a starter, and three managers discover the waiver wire is a spiritual discipline. Be kind, set your lineups, and remember that nobody has lost a single matchup yet. League-wide draft grade: A-. Deep rosters, a handful of beautiful turns, and just enough center hoarding to keep us all entertained until spring.
